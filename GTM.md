# GTM

The exact target user is a founder-operator, finance lead, or engineering manager at a 10-80 person AI-forward startup that has accumulated paid seats across ChatGPT, Claude, Cursor, Copilot, Gemini, and API dashboards without a clean owner for spend. The best initial persona is a head of engineering who still approves software renewals and feels the pain of every engineer quietly adding another AI tool.

Right before they want StackTrim, they are searching or scrolling through posts about "AI tool sprawl," "Cursor vs Copilot cost," "Claude Team minimum seats," "OpenAI credits," or "why is our Anthropic bill so high." They are not looking for procurement software. They are looking for a fast sanity check they can send to a founder or finance partner.

They hang out in specific places: Hacker News launch threads about developer tools, r/SaaS, r/startups, r/ExperiencedDevs, Indie Hackers, AI Engineer World's community, Latent Space Discord, Cursor and Claude Code discussions on X, and founder/operator Slack groups like Hampton, Rands Leadership, and local founder groups. The strongest wedge is not generic SEO; it is replying to real conversations where teams are already comparing AI subscriptions.

First 100 users in 30 days with $0 paid budget: launch a teardown-style post titled "I audited 12 AI stacks and found the same three wastes" with anonymized examples, then include StackTrim as the free calculator. Post the findings to Hacker News as a Show HN, write a short X thread with screenshots of three audit outcomes, and personally DM 50 founders/engineering leads with a one-line offer: "send me your AI tools, I will run a public-safe audit and give you the link." Turn the best 10 audits into anonymized mini case studies. Ask each respondent to share their public report if it found more than $250/month.

The unfair distribution channel for Credex is credit advisory. Credex can pair the calculator with real knowledge about AI credits, vendor programs, and procurement leverage that generic SaaS spend tools do not have. The tool should not just say "spend less"; it should say "this $8k/year waste could become credits or runway."

Week-1 traction if it works: 250 unique visitors, 80 completed audits, 25 captured emails, 8 reports shared publicly, and 3 Credex consultation bookings from high-savings audits. The key signal is not raw traffic. It is whether people trust the report enough to enter spend numbers and share the result.
